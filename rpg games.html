Originating from tabletop games like Dungeons and Dragons, you assume the identity of a specific character (or a party of characters) and customize their skills, equipment, and personality traits. As you complete tasks, you earn experience points (XP) to make your character objectively stronger.

Their Focus: Character growth, statistics, and narrative decisions.

Some exaples of the genres:
  • Action RPGs: Combat happens in real-time, testing your physical reflexes alongside your character’s stats.
  • Tactical RPGs: Combat plays out like a complex game of chess on a grid, where positioning and turn-by-turn math matter more than quick reflexes.
  • MMORPGs (Massively Multiplayer Online): Persistent digital worlds where thousands of real players interact, trade, and go on quests together simultaneously.
  • Roguelikes: Games with randomly generated levels where death is permanent. When you die, you lose your progress and have to start over from the very beginning, often using what you learned to get a little further next time.
